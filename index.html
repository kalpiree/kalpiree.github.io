I am a PhD candidate in Artificial Intelligence at the University of Technology Sydney and a visiting researcher at The Education University of Hong Kong. I expect to complete my PhD in April 2027.

My research focuses on the statistical foundations of trustworthy machine learning, with applications to recommender systems and large language models. I am particularly interested in uncertainty quantification, statistical risk control, and continual learning under distribution shifts.

Selected work

All publications

ALIGN-Rec: Continual Recommendation under Heterogeneous Unlearning Requests

Nitin Bisht, Sumit Bisht, Tong Zhang, Yu Yang, Huan Huo, Guandong Xu

NeurIPS 2026Paper

When a recommender keeps learning, information a user asked it to forget can start influencing its predictions again. ALIGN-Rec controls later updates so the system can learn from new activity while limiting the return of deleted information, without retraining from scratch.

CARE: Adaptive Calibration for Reliable Recommendations

Nitin Bisht, Huan Huo, Xiuwen Gong, Guandong Xu

ICML 2026Paper

People's interests change, so recommendations can become less reliable after a model is deployed. CARE tracks these changes and adjusts how many items it recommends, keeping lists compact with statistical guarantees on recommendation quality.

FRAG: Fair Retrieval-Augmented Generation for LLM-based Sequential Recommendation

Tong Zhang, Nitin Bisht, Zihao Li, Xianzhi Wang, Xiuwen Gong, Guandong Xu

WSDM 2027·Accepted for oral presentation

LLM recommenders can repeatedly give some groups of items too little visibility. FRAG tracks this imbalance and changes which items the model considers next, improving exposure for overlooked groups while keeping recommendations relevant.

MIDA: Multi-Interest Decoding with Adaptive Aggregation for Session-based Recommendation

Linjiang Guo*, Nitin Bisht*, Shiqing Wu, Guandong Xu

IEEE TKDE · Accepted September 2026·Co-first authorPaper DOI

Longer browsing sessions can make recommendations worse when models focus too heavily on recent clicks. MIDA identifies the different interests across a session and combines earlier choices with recent activity to improve the next recommendation.
